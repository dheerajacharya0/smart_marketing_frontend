# Billing disputes, not bugs, drive WhatsApp SaaS complaints

Across eleven competitor platforms — Interakt, WATI, AiSensy, Gallabox, Zoko, Twilio, 360dialog, Gupshup, Yellow.ai, Respond.io, and Trengo — the single most frequent and severe support-ticket category is **billing and refund disputes**, not product bugs or outages. Users on review sites describe refunds refused outright, converted to in-platform credit instead of cash, or stalled for weeks while automatic renewals keep charging; WATI alone has multiple dated Trustpilot reviews describing $950 and $50 charges the company would not reverse ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wati.io)). The second most common complaint, which frequently co-occurs with the first, is **slow or unresponsive customer support** — days-long waits, unhelpful bots, and tickets that sit open through active outages. Layered beneath both is a **structural, cross-vendor category**: WhatsApp number bans, template rejections, Business Manager verification failures, and the opaque "quality rating" system are Meta's own enforcement mechanisms, but because Meta offers no direct consumer support channel, every one of these frustrations lands as a support ticket against whichever SaaS tool the business happens to use. A fourth, moderately evidenced theme is markup or pricing-model opacity layered on top of Meta's own fees, and a fifth, weaker but real theme is platform downtime, migration lock-in, and product-specific gaps (dashboards, chatbot NLU, onboarding complexity). The research base itself has a notable skew: review-aggregator scores diverge sharply by site (Twilio shows 4.1/5 on G2 but roughly 1/5 on Trustpilot from 877+ reviews), meaning severity rankings below should be read as "what angry customers write down," not as representative of the median user experience.

## Billing, refunds, and surprise renewals are the most universal complaint

Every Indian BSP-layer platform examined — WATI, Interakt, and AiSensy — shows the same pattern: refund requests denied or converted to credit, cancellations that take weeks to process while billing continues, and plan downgrades that still trigger full annual renewal charges. WATI reviewer Assaf Manor described a plan downgrade that still "defaults to charging your card for another full $950 annual renewal," with the offered refund paid in platform credit rather than cash ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wati.io)); another WATI user, Ronnie Teja, had a refund refused just two days after signup ("we are not going to refund you"), and a third, dennis smits, reported a cancellation that took roughly two months while billing continued ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wati.io)). Interakt shows the identical shape: reviewer Kai zad wrote that "they will not refund but will adjust your money in a future plan," and a Shopify-app reviewer (Anish Suri) said a refund request had been outstanding for 10 days with product access blocked during the dispute ([AppNavigator](https://appnavigator.io/app/interakt-marketing/reviews/?rating=1)); other Interakt Shopify merchants reported accounts "automatically deactivated without any warning" followed by double billing, and subscription fees charged after uninstalling the app ([AppNavigator](https://appnavigator.io/app/interakt-sales/reviews/?rating=1)). AiSensy's own FAQ exists specifically to explain its unusual "timestamp-to-timestamp" (not calendar-month) billing cycle, which is itself evidence that this structure generates confusion tickets ([AiSensy FAQ](https://faq.aisensy.com/aisensy-billing-cycle-whatsapp-pricing-details)), and a Trustpilot reviewer separately called the service "a complete scam" after paying over $150 for software that never worked.

Twilio shows the same complaint type at far larger scale and severity: Trustpilot reviewers describe accounts "blocked or suspended without clear reasons," sometimes after funds had already been added, "inexplicable charges for services not used," and outright refund refusals, contributing to a TrustScore of roughly **1/5 across 877–911 reviews** even though Twilio's G2 score sits at a healthy 4.1/5 ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/twilio.com?page=5); [G2](https://g2.com/products/twilio/reviews?page=2)). Trengo and Respond.io show a related but distinct variant — cost *unpredictability* rather than refund refusal: a Trengo Capterra reviewer described "multiple sudden changes of price, very unstable (more than doubled)" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/177967/Trengo/reviews)), while Respond.io's G2 cons list "Expensive" as its single largest complaint bucket at 14 reviews, compounded by a Monthly-Active-Contacts pricing model that steps up sharply at 1,000/2,500/5,000/10,000-contact tiers ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/kustomer-vs-respond-io); [prospeo.io](https://prospeo.io/s/respondio-pricing-reviews-pros-and-cons)). Gallabox and Zoko are comparative outliers with no billing-dispute pattern surfacing in their (admittedly thin) review samples, and Zoko explicitly advertises passing Meta's fees through without markup — a claim corroborated by its own published per-conversation platform fee structure rather than contradicted by user reviews ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zoko-review)).

## Support response time is the complaint that compounds every other one

Slow or absent support shows up on nearly every platform's review pages, and the pattern that recurs most is specifically *billing problems paired with unreachable support* — users don't just have a dispute, they have no way to resolve it. WATI reviewers describe response times of "two or three days" and cases where "it will take days to sort out issues, even if the issue is halting your business" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wati.io)); Interakt Shopify merchants described "an entire week" to resolve issues and an AI bot that "just keeps saying nobody is available to chat" ([AppNavigator](https://appnavigator.io/app/interakt-sales/reviews/?rating=1)); and AiSensy support reportedly took "over two weeks" to respond on one Shopify app ticket. 360dialog's Trustpilot page carries some of the sharpest language in the entire dataset: reviewer Tristan described being "left stranded for 48+hrs after their service went down" with multiple agents unable to help, and Rajesh Watts reported support as "nonexistent," with a billing dispute unresolved after "ONE MONTH" ([Trustpilot DE](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/360dialog.com?page=2)) — notable because 360dialog's Software Advice support sub-score (3.38) is its lowest category and roughly 31% of its reviews are one-star ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/ipaas/360dialog-profile)). Twilio's G2 reviews include a small-business customer who got no response for "over 2 weeks" after an account suspension and another who received "the same verification request from three different support representatives" ([G2](https://g2.com/products/twilio/reviews_and_filters?page=5)). Yellow.ai's complaint profile adds "account-team turnover" to slow response times, consistent across G2, Trustpilot, and Gartner Peer Insights aggregation ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/yellow-ai-reviews)), while Gupshup and Gallabox show milder versions of the same theme — "Poor Customer Support" and "Dashboard Issues" as G2's top negative tags for Gupshup, and a GetApp reviewer calling Gallabox's service "the worst," saying "there is no customer support" ([G2](https://g2.com/products/gupshup/reviews); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2052589/gallabox)).

## Meta's own enforcement layer — bans, rejections, verification — generates tickets at every vendor regardless of fault

The third-ranked but arguably most structurally important complaint category is one no SaaS vendor actually controls: WhatsApp number bans, template rejections, Business Manager verification failures, and quality-rating throttling are Meta-side decisions, yet because Meta provides no direct consumer support line, every one of these becomes a ticket against the BSP or SaaS layer in between. WATI users reported Meta-side account restrictions directly tied to their onboarding — Thayatharn Deivendran said connecting WATI led to "one of our telephone numbers was permanently blocked," and Alejandro reported a number blocked after bulk messaging with "no way to cancel directly through the website" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wati.io)). A LinkedIn poster described a WhatsApp Business number "built and operated for over 7 years" being "permanently banned without a specific explanation or prior warning" ([LinkedIn via search aggregation](https://in.linkedin.com/in/hkneotia)), and a Meta developer community thread complained there is "no proper support from Meta" and bug reporting is "restricted with a limit" ([Meta developer community](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/community/threads/1168507490902722)). Official WhatsApp error codes for this are explicit — 131044, 131048, 131056 — and one help center distinguishes a usually-permanent "Banned" status from an appealable "Restricted" one ([PickyAssist](https://help.pickyassist.com/setting-up-guide/whatsapp-tips-and-tricks/whatsapp-number-blocked-or-banned-what-to-do)); Meta's own platform-wide enforcement is large-scale, with reported bans of **6.6 million accounts in India in May 2024** and **8.4 million in August 2024** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/whatsapp-blocked-84-5-lakh-indian-accounts-in-august-2024/amp)).

Template rejection follows the same pattern. Interakt's Saree Tales review described two months of rejected templates, including Interakt's own built-in ones ([AppNavigator](https://appnavigator.io/app/interakt-sales/reviews/?rating=1)); Twilio's error 63041 pauses templates for "recurring negative customer feedback," and Twilio's own support documentation states plainly that "Meta's decision on template appeals is final and Twilio cannot affect the final decision" ([Twilio docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/63041); [Twilio support](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/37014510989979)). 360dialog's documentation adds a concrete technical friction point on top of Meta's review: edited templates can be resubmitted only once per 24 hours or up to 10 times per 30 days ([360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/messaging/template-messages/template-statuses)). Business verification and Embedded Signup show the identical black-box pattern — a Meta developer reported a "This app needs at least one supported permission" error despite correct configuration, and a dev.to guide attributes frequent "not eligible" errors to Meta's opaque credibility checks on newly registered numbers ([Meta developer community](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/1070172575755746/); [dev.to](https://dev.to/duochatio/whatsapp-coexistence-embedded-signup-issues-solutions-duochat-1aho)). The "quality rating" system compounds this: one vendor's own commentary concedes the signal "is aggregated and not traceable to a single message," and businesses typically "only discover this when something goes wrong — a campaign underperforms, templates get paused, or a message limit stops climbing for no obvious reason" (sources unisolated at URL level in the underlying search). Meta's July 1, 2025 shift from conversation-based to per-message pricing is a final cross-cutting driver of billing-shock tickets, confirmed on Meta's own developer documentation, with a Meta developer community thread showing direct backlash over the loss of the previously free 24-hour service window ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing); [Meta developer community](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/1328420532772816/?locale=ar_AR)).

## Pricing markup and migration lock-in are real but secondary frictions

A fourth, moderately-evidenced theme is the markup BSPs charge on top of Meta's own per-message or per-conversation fees, which creates "why am I being double-charged" tickets distinct from Meta's pricing itself. A 360dialog Trustpilot reviewer, Rajesh Watts, asked directly "why do you keep on charging double when Meta is already charging us," which sits in direct tension with 360dialog's own marketing claim of a "transparent flat-fee price — with no markup" ([Trustpilot DE](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/360dialog.com?page=2)). Gupshup's support documentation confirms a separate "Gupshup fee for Cloud API marketing" that is "charged on delivery" and "sits outside capping plans" — appearing as its own line item rather than folded into predictable plan pricing ([Gupshup support](https://support.gupshup.io/hc/en-us/articles/53700207494169)). AiSensy's G2 reviews quantify a markup of 88 paisa versus Meta's 75 paise per marketing conversation, and G2 separately estimates AiSensy as priced "30% more expensive than the average for chatbot products" ([G2 via search summary](https://www.g2.com/products/aisensy/reviews)). Respond.io's own blog states candidly that it stopped supporting third-party BSPs, including 360dialog, because "many WhatsApp BSPs impose unfair charges on customers, including markups, flat fees and per-message fees" — a notable admission from a company that itself operates in this market ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/respondio-no-longer-supports-other-bsps-heres-why)).

Migration and lock-in friction is the fifth theme, evidenced mainly for 360dialog: a Trustpilot reviewer ("Durazno") reported the self-service migration feature "does not work," with no useful support guidance, and 360dialog's own documentation confirms migrating a number requires matching Business Manager IDs, disabling two-factor authentication on the source side (a 48–72 hour support-ticket process for BSP-owned WABAs), and re-verifying via SMS code ([Trustpilot DE](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/360dialog.com?page=2); [360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/waba-basics/migrating-phone-numbers/migrate-a-number-to-360dialog)). AiSensy shows a milder version: a competitor's migration guide notes numbers can stay "tied to an old AiSensy project" after cancellation, needing 1–2 business days for AiSensy to release ([Spur help center](https://help.spurnow.com/en/articles/12114738-migrating-from-aisensy-to-spur-why-you-need-to-release-your-number)). Platform downtime rounds out the evidenced themes but at lower frequency: 360dialog logged a ~19-hour webhook-delivery outage and separate status trackers disagree on exact incident counts ([IsDown](https://isdown.app/status/360dialog/incidents/296583-webhook-events-not-arriving-for-on-prem-numbers)); Twilio's status history includes a January 2025 outbound-WhatsApp failure (error 63005, ~5 hours) and an October 2024 Meta-linked delivery problem leaving messages stuck in "SENT" status ([StatusGator](https://statusgator.com/blog/twilio-outage-history/)); and Trengo logged a 21-hour "WhatsApp Sending" outage in September 2025 ([isdown.app](https://isdown.app/status/trengo/incidents/452489-whatsapp-sending)). Product-specific gaps appear narrowly: Yellow.ai draws "Chatbot Limitations" and weaker NLU scores (8.3/10 vs. Kore.ai's 9.2/10) consistent with its heavier AI positioning ([G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare/rasa-vs-yellow-ai-vs-kore-ai)), while Gallabox and Gupshup show milder "Dashboard Issues" and "Inadequate Reporting" complaints on small review samples ([G2 Gallabox](https://g2.com/products/gallabox/reviews_and_filters?page=9)).

## Conclusion

The ranking that emerges is consistent across all eleven platforms and the cross-cutting Meta layer: money (refunds, surprise renewals, markup) ranks first, the inability to get timely help when money or access breaks ranks second, and Meta's own opaque enforcement machinery (bans, template rejections, verification, quality-rating throttling, and its 2025 pricing shift) ranks third but is arguably the most consequential because it is structurally unfixable by any vendor and generates tickets regardless of which platform a business chose. The practical implication is that "product quality" — chatbot accuracy, dashboard polish, API reliability — is a distant fourth-and-fifth-place concern in what actually drives negative reviews; users rarely leave a platform over a missing feature, but reliably leave scathing reviews over an unresolved bill or an unexplained ban. A second, important caveat for anyone using this ranking to prioritize support investment: review-site skew is severe and uneven (Twilio's ~80-point gap between G2 and Trustpilot is the starkest example), and this research pass was structurally unable to retrieve genuine Reddit, Twitter/X, or Hacker News discussion for any of the eleven platforms despite repeated targeted queries — meaning the written record here likely overweights the small subset of users angry enough to post on a review-aggregator site, while underweighting silent churn and informal social complaints that never got worded into a star rating.
