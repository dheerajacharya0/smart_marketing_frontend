# Every WhatsApp BSP hits the same wall

No WhatsApp Business Solution Provider surveyed — 360dialog, Wati, Gupshup, Twilio, Interakt, Infobip, Gallabox, Kapso, Glassix, eGrow, ManyChat, Make, or Zoho — has built anything that detects a stale-WABA conflict before the customer hits Embedded Signup's rejection, nor anything that resolves it without the customer manually deleting the old entry themselves on a Meta-hosted surface. This holds whether the stale WABA sits under the same customer's own forgotten Business Manager or under a completely unrelated one. The honest conclusion across all three research passes is that Converszio's diagnosed failure — a silent rejection with no usable error signal, traceable only by reading Meta's internal GraphQL traffic in DevTools — is a *worse* variant of an industry-wide, structurally unsolved problem, not a Converszio-specific gap. No competitor offers proactive pre-flight detection, no competitor offers a deep-linked or automated remediation tool, and no first-hand account anywhere in the public record shows Meta or a BSP fixing this server-side without the account owner taking a manual action. The one place a real, buildable gap exists is not technical remediation but **honesty and structure in how the dead end is communicated** — something every BSP surveyed does only partially, and none does for the single worst sub-case: a customer who doesn't personally admin the business holding the stale WABA.

## Nobody checks before the popup opens — the whole industry is reactive by default

Every BSP's documented "number check" step is either a self-reported radio button inside the signup flow itself or a manual instruction to the customer ("send yourself a test WhatsApp message," "look in the WhatsApp Business app for a Business Account label") — not a system-run lookup against Meta's data run from the BSP's own dashboard before Embedded Signup opens. 360dialog's "Confirm Number details" step is a self-selection among options, not a dashboard-run query, and its only concrete "how to check" instruction is sending a test message ([360dialog: Confirm Number details](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/hub/embedded-signup/confirm-number-details.md)). Wati and Interakt both tell the customer to delete any existing WhatsApp account *themselves* before signup, with no pre-check tool offered ([Wati: choosing the right phone number](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/11463160-choosing-the-right-phone-number-for-whatsapp); [Interakt resource center](https://interakt.shop/?p=910)). Twilio frames the entire situation as a post-hoc error (Error 63110) diagnosed only after a failed registration attempt, never a pre-flight check ([Twilio: Guided Onboarding](https://www.twilio.com/docs/whatsapp/guided-onboarding)). This isn't a documentation gap that happens to obscure a hidden feature — it tracks the actual architecture of Meta's Graph API. Every documented endpoint for phone numbers and WABAs (`GET /<WABA_ID>/phone_numbers`, `GET /<PHONE_NUMBER_ID>`) is scoped to a WABA the caller's access token already has a grant on; there is no documented endpoint letting any third party ask "is this arbitrary number already registered to some WABA I don't manage" ([Meta: Client phone numbers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/solution-providers/manage-phone-numbers.md)). The one eligibility-style endpoint found in BSP documentation — 360dialog's Meta Business Agent "Check Eligibility" call — checks a different product entirely (AI-agent messaging setup) and is still scoped to a WABA the caller already manages, so it's not evidence that Meta is willing to expose this specific cross-business lookup ([360dialog: MBA Check Eligibility](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/mba/meta-business-agent/1.-check-eligibility)). The structural reason is permissions, not laziness: Meta's internal `xfb_check_pn_first_phone_eligibility` call (the one Converszio observed in the popup's own network traffic) almost certainly works only because it runs with Meta's own first-party privilege inside a Meta-controlled surface — no public, app-token-scoped equivalent exists for any BSP to call, and nothing in the public record suggests Meta is building one.

## Remediation tooling tops out at "pick your WABA from a dropdown"

No BSP constructs a pre-filled deep link into Meta Business Suite or WhatsApp Manager pointing at the specific contested phone number or WABA — every doc checked (360dialog, eGrow, Gallabox, Infobip, Kapso) gives prose navigation instructions or a generic support-ticket link, never a constructed URL ([360dialog migration docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/hub/migrations/moving-numbers-between-business-managers); [eGrow help article](https://help.egrow.com/es/article/this-number-is-registered-to-an-existing-whatsapp-business-account-on-another-business-solution-provider)). This isn't an oversight either: 360dialog's own documentation warns that the `business_id` parameter visible in a Business Manager URL reflects whichever Business Manager the viewer is *currently in*, not the one that owns the contested WABA — so even a BSP that tried to build this would be constructing a link that can't target an asset it doesn't control ([360dialog: Meta Business Manager](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/waba-basics/meta-business-manager)). On diagnostic capture, 360dialog's support-ticket flow is the single best thing found anywhere in this research: customers pick the WABA and phone number from structured dropdown fields rather than a free-text box ([360dialog: Reaching out to support](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/~/changes/tKKxKoPU2ghkZCHDgiXZ/help-and-support/reaching-out-to-support)). But it's still customer-entered data, not anything auto-captured from the failure itself — no BSP surveyed (Gallabox, Kapso, Infobip, Gupshup) auto-attaches an app ID, error code, or session ID to a ticket, and none auto-forwards a case to Meta on the customer's behalf. 360dialog does publish concrete first-response targets by topic (Embedded Signup issues: 8 hours; Number Registration: 8 hours; Messaging problems: 4 hours), with an explicit admission that Meta-dependent processes carry no SLA at all and only escalate to Meta's Trust & Safety team after 30 days stuck ([360dialog: Reaching out to support](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/~/changes/tKKxKoPU2ghkZCHDgiXZ/help-and-support/reaching-out-to-support)) — this is the most honest piece of expectation-setting found anywhere in the survey, and it's about *response*, not *resolution*. No BSP was found marketing a paid "concierge" or "white-glove" tier scoped specifically to Meta-side account conflicts; the closest evidence is one unverified Shopify review describing extended Zoko onboarding support and a 360dialog mention of migration assistance "on request" for high-volume enterprise numbers — neither is a documented product with resolution-time guarantees for this failure class.

## Meta's own support channel is frequently a dead end, and BSPs don't bridge it

The strongest, most consistent finding across every first-hand account surveyed is that Meta's direct support path for this exact failure class is either slow, auto-closed, or structurally unreachable — and no BSP demonstrably does better. A Tech Provider stuck with a number in coexistence mode from a prior BSP found the bug-report form wouldn't submit and was blocked from Meta's Direct Support portal entirely because the affected WABA sat under a different Business Manager than his own account — he never reached a human ([Meta Developer Community thread #1635723110883422](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/1635723110883422/)). A separate thread documents Direct Support auto-closing WABA escalations within seconds via an automated "AI Agent" that claims routing to a human specialist but never delivers one ([Meta Developer Community thread #1431949575481512](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/1431949575481512/)). A Make.com user stuck for over four months was told by Meta support to "find a BSP," despite having none — and Make's own moderator confirmed plainly this was "a Meta restriction and Make could not help" ([Make Community thread](https://community.make.com/t/phone-number-stuck-in-test-waba-after-disconnecting-make-how-to-release-it/100482)). No first-hand account anywhere in this research shows a BSP successfully using a partner or account-manager relationship to get a stuck number released faster than the customer could have done alone — the pattern in public BSP community replies (ManyChat, Make) is support staff characterizing the problem as Meta's, outside their control, rather than demonstrating an internal escalation channel that works. Critically, even manual self-deletion — the fix that worked for Converszio — is not reliably sufficient: a two-year-spanning Meta Developer Community thread shows five separate reporters who correctly deleted a number from its old WABA, confirmed via a `wa.me` check that it was free, and still hit the same "registered on a WhatsApp Business account" error on re-registration, with SMS verification failing outright and **no resolution ever confirmed by anyone in the thread** ([Meta Developer Community thread #625477906497263](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/community/threads/625477906497263/)). This is the single most important real-world data point for setting customer expectations: the manual fix is necessary in essentially every reported case, but it is not a guaranteed cure, and Meta's backend can remain inconsistent even after the customer does everything asked of them correctly.

## The verdict: no technical edge exists, only a communication edge

Pulling the three threads together, there is no evidence anywhere in this research that any competitor has solved — or even meaningfully mitigated — this failure mode better than Converszio's current manual-deletion fix. Every BSP is reactive, not proactive (no pre-flight check exists or could exist against Meta's current API surface); every BSP's remediation tooling tops out at structured support-ticket intake, not automation; and Meta's own support channel is documented as unreliable regardless of which BSP sits in front of it. That means the genuinely available differentiator is not a workaround nobody has found — it's being more transparent and structured about an unsolved problem than competitors currently are. Three specific things would be real, novel improvements, not reinvention of something competitors already do: first, **auto-capturing diagnostic context at the moment of failure** (error code, session ID, WABA/business identifiers where the payload exposes them) rather than asking the customer to self-report them into a free-text or even a dropdown ticket field — no BSP surveyed does this, including 360dialog, whose dropdown-based intake is the closest anyone gets; second, **honest, two-sided expectation-setting that states resolution isn't guaranteed even after the correct manual step** — every BSP found states the fix ("disconnect it") but none disclose, the way the two-year Meta thread's experience would justify, that deletion sometimes doesn't work and Meta's backend state can simply stay broken with no stated recourse; third, **a documented, named fallback for the specific sub-case of a customer who does not personally administer the business holding the stale WABA** — every BSP's instructions implicitly assume the customer can act (delete it themselves), and the one BSP guide that addresses the different-BSP case (Glassix) punts the ask to "contact the old BSP," a path with zero confirmed successes anywhere in this research. Converszio should write that third case down as what it is — a dead end with no self-serve path and no confirmed success by anyone in the public record — rather than implying a fix exists. Conversely, two things are *not* worth building because they would just be reinventing what the record shows doesn't work or doesn't matter: a pre-filled deep link into Business Suite (confirmed structurally impossible, since the `business_id` in any URL reflects the viewer's current Business Manager, not the target one an unrelated party would need to act on), and a "contact Meta for you" escalation feature (no evidence any BSP's version of this outperforms the customer going direct, and strong evidence Meta's own portal frequently refuses or auto-closes these cases regardless of who files them).

## Conclusion

The research closes the loop opened by the prior "Automating stale number cleanup" finding: not only is there no API to automate the fix, there is no competitor anywhere in the BSP landscape who has found a materially better path around the same wall. That reframes the product question entirely — this is not a feature-parity gap to close, it's a trust-and-transparency gap to win. The defensible position for Converszio is to be the BSP that tells customers, precisely and in advance, which of three distinct Meta-side states applies to them, captures what diagnostic context actually exists automatically instead of asking them to hunt for it, and says plainly — contrary to every other BSP's framing — that the fix sometimes doesn't take even when done correctly, and that a customer who isn't personally an admin of the stale business has no confirmed path to resolution at all. No one else is saying that last part out loud; saying it is the actual competitive edge available here.
