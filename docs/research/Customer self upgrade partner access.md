# Let the real owner click "grant access"

The customer who ran the interrupted Embedded Signup is very likely the Admin of the auto-created "Digital help" Business by ordinary Meta mechanics — whoever completes a no-existing-business signup becomes that portfolio's first Admin — but **our app cannot see or act on that itself**, because Standard vs. Advanced Access is scoped to the calling app's relationship with a user or business, not to the target business's actual admin roster; our Tech Provider System User's partner-level grant on the WABA is structurally incapable of performing delete/edit/settings actions regardless of what the customer's own Facebook login can do. The fix Meta actually built for this is a human, UI-only action: the owner opens Business Settings → Accounts → WhatsApp Accounts → the WABA → Partners, and re-assigns our Business Portfolio ID with "Full control" checked — a documented Graph API equivalent (`POST /{WABA_ID}/assigned_users`) exists but requires a System User admin token we don't have and the customer shouldn't hand us, so the UI click is the only realistic lever. Two BSPs, 360dialog and Wati, ship exactly this as a standalone "re-grant access" recovery flow (not just onboarding), both triggered from a button in their own product that re-invokes Embedded Signup or deep-links to the Partners tab — direct precedent for building this as a sibling to our existing OTP-based number-recovery dialog. Business Verification is a plausible complementary fix for a *related* failure (unverified owners can fail to complete sharing at all) but no source shows it retroactively fixing an *already-shared* WABA's insufficient task grant, so it should be offered as a second, optional step, not the primary fix. Before writing a line of this flow, the one irreplaceable step is logging into business.facebook.com as the actual customer and checking, on the real "Digital help" account, whether they appear as Admin and whether the Partners tab even presents a materially different state to grant — everything else here is documentation-grade confidence, not confirmed-on-this-account confidence.

## The customer probably owns "Digital help" — and our app is structurally blind to it

Meta's Embedded Signup FAQ confirms the flow lets a user "create a new" business portfolio when they don't select an existing one, and states that customers onboarded this way "own all of their WhatsApp assets" with unrestrictable full access to WhatsApp Manager ([Meta Embedded Signup FAQ](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/embedded-signup/faq)). Meta never spells out, in one explicit sentence, "the signing-in user becomes that new portfolio's Admin," but this is the standard behavior of Business Manager creation everywhere else on the platform, and BSP documentation converges on the same expectation: Verloop's Embedded Signup integration notes that the business picker actively excludes businesses "where the logged-in user isn't an admin" ([Verloop](https://help.verloop.io/?p=2327)), and Bird's explainer instructs that whoever runs the flow should already be "the business portfolio owner or an admin with rights over its WhatsApp assets" ([Bird](https://bird.com/explained/whatsapp/how-does-whatsapp-embedded-signup-work.md)). "Digital help" — unverified, no address, no payment method, a generic placeholder name rather than something a customer would deliberately choose — matches the fingerprint of a bare-minimum portfolio auto-spawned by this no-existing-business branch, not a pre-existing third party with no relationship to the customer. The loss of `window.opener` that aborted the flow client-side is a popup-communication failure between the browser and our page; nothing found suggests it also interrupts Meta's own server-side creation of the Business and admin assignment, which is why the working theory — the customer already is Digital help's Admin and doesn't know it — is the strongest read of the evidence, while remaining formally unconfirmed.

The reason our own backend can't simply check this and act on it is Meta's **Access Levels** mechanism, and it's worth being precise about what it does and doesn't gate. Meta's Graph API documentation states Access Levels are "a new Graph API authorization mechanism for Business apps" where **Standard Access** "only allows access to data owned by users who have a Role on the app, or a Role in a Business that has claimed the app," while **Advanced Access** "allows access to data owned by any app user, but requires App Review" ([Graph API Changelog v8.0](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/version8.0); [Access Levels](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/overview/access-levels/)). The critical, easily-missed detail: this is a property of the **calling app's relationship to the user**, not of the **user's relationship to the target business**. The customer being Admin of "Digital help" does nothing for our app's Standard Access unless the customer also has a role on our app or "Digital help" has claimed our app — neither is true in a normal self-serve signup. That's precisely why our System User gets error #100 even though, in principle, "the right person" is sitting right there, logged into our dashboard: our app is the wrong instrument to ask the question through. It also means this restriction is a dead end only for *our app's own Graph API calls* — it says nothing about the customer acting through Meta's own first-party surfaces (the Business Settings web UI, or their own fresh OAuth token in something like Graph API Explorer), which are evaluated on the customer's actual Business role, full stop, not on our app's access tier. No source states this as a single explicit sentence from Meta — it's a structurally sound inference from how Access Levels are documented, not a verbatim quote — but it is the load-bearing fact that makes a customer-side self-service flow even conceivable: the permission gap lives entirely in our app-to-Meta relationship, and the customer's own login sidesteps it completely rather than needing to fix it.

## Granting access is a UI click for the owner, not an API call we can trigger

Meta's documented, current path for an owner to grant or upgrade a partner's access on a WABA is **Business Settings → Accounts → WhatsApp Accounts → select the WABA → Partners tab → Assign Partner**, entering the partner's Business Portfolio ID and choosing a permission level, with "Grant full control / Everything" as the top tier ([360dialog Asset Sharing](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/asset-sharing)). A second Meta developer page corroborates the same flow — check the WABA, "use the toggles to define which permissions to grant," click Assign — without naming the toggles explicitly ([WhatsApp Business Accounts](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/whatsapp-business-accounts)), and removal is the mirror action: Partners tab → select partner → delete icon ([Migrating WABAs Among Solution Partners](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/solution-providers/support/migrating-wabas-among-solution-partners-via-embedded-signup)). This is the one lever a human customer, with nothing but their own Facebook login, can pull directly — and it requires no token exchange with us at all.

The Graph API equivalent exists and is well-documented — `POST /{WHATSAPP_BUSINESS_ACCOUNT_ID}/assigned_users` with `user` (a system-user ID) and `tasks` (e.g. `['MANAGE']`) — but every primary source is explicit that the caller needs **"the access token of a System User with admin permissions,"** not a plain personal/owner access token ([Manage System Users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/solution-providers/manage-system-users/); [Embedded Signup — System Users](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/embedded-signup/manage-accounts/system-users)). `MANAGE` itself is qualified further — it "will only work if you are given full access on the solution i.e. including `MESSAGING`" — meaning the fix most likely to actually close the delete/edit gap is upgrading the **Messaging-account-tier** access (Standard → Admin), not merely adding `MANAGE_TEMPLATES` to a granular task list that was never confirmed to cover delete in the first place. This is the key practical conclusion for deciding which path to build: asking the customer to hand over a System User admin token is a materially bigger and riskier ask than a one-time UI click, and no source confirms a plain personal access token can even call this endpoint — so **the UI path, not the API path, is the only one usable by a human customer**, and it should be the entire shape of the flow we build. One real unknown survives research and needs a live check: whether re-sharing with "Full control" on a WABA that already has *some* partner access (ours, partial) is additive/upgrading or requires removing the partner first — no Meta or BSP source answers this, and 360dialog elsewhere warns that removing access before confirming a replacement grant can lock everyone out, so the safest default instruction is "select Full control and Assign," never "remove first."

## Two BSPs already ship this as a recovery flow, and verification solves a different problem

This is not a novel ask. 360dialog maintains a standalone troubleshooting page, separate from onboarding, titled around "BSP Permission" that states "For messaging to resume, the Asset must be re-shared with 360dialog," giving the customer the same Partners-tab click-path as a **named recovery step**, with 360dialog's own Business Partner ID pre-identified for them, or alternatively a "Fix channel setup" button that re-runs Embedded Signup against the existing WABA ([360dialog BSP Permission](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/waba-basics/bsp-permission)). Wati ships the direct analog: "How to add Wati back under Partners in Meta Business Manager," explicit that "these steps are only required if Wati was removed by mistake," walking the customer through Partners → Assign Partner → Wati's Business Manager ID → Full control → Assign ([Wati](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/13106135-how-to-add-wati-back-under-partners-in-meta-business-manager)). Twilio's error-code documentation for a broken partner invitation (63020) likewise tells the customer directly to accept the pending invite and confirm verification in Business Manager ([Twilio Error 63020](https://static0.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/63020)). The caveat worth carrying into design: every documented case is **binary** — access was fully removed or never granted, then restored — not the narrower "upgrade an existing-but-insufficient partial grant" scenario we're actually in. No BSP documents that specific nuance, which means our customer-facing copy should say "re-grant full access" rather than "upgrade your prior grant," since Meta's own UI likely doesn't distinguish the two anyway — re-assigning with Full control checked is probably the same action regardless of what partial state preceded it. Also material: both 360dialog and Wati explicitly carve out WhatsApp Business app **Coexistence** numbers as unfixable by this click-path — those require a full Embedded Signup redo, not a Partners-tab edit — so before shipping, we should confirm "Digital help"'s WABA isn't a coexistence number.

Business Verification is a real, documented lever, but for an adjacent failure. Sent's troubleshooting docs state plainly that "Meta requires business verification before you can share your WhatsApp Business Account with third-party partners," naming unverified status as a cause of a sharing handshake *failing to complete in the first place* ([Sent.dm](https://docs.sent.dm/troubleshooting/whatsapp-setup)). That's a plausible origin story for why "Digital help" ended up with a partial, partner-only grant rather than a full one — an unverified-business block mid-handshake during the original interrupted signup — but no source anywhere confirms verification *retroactively* fixes a WABA that is *already* partner-shared; it reads as a prerequisite for a clean sharing event, not a cure for one that already went sideways. Verification is also gated to the owner alone: 360dialog states documents "must be uploaded exclusively by an authorized representative of the Business," explicitly barring partners/Tech Providers from doing it on the customer's behalf ([360dialog, via search summary of Meta Business Verification docs]), and it requires legal documents matching the Business's registered name and address — a real risk if "Digital help" carries placeholder metadata the customer has no paperwork for. The practical sequencing this suggests: offer the Partners-tab re-grant as the primary, fast, low-friction fix, and offer verification as a secondary step to make the grant durable and to unlock unrelated scale ceilings (250 vs. 6,000 template cap, messaging limits) — not as a substitute for the re-grant itself.

## Build it as a guided re-grant, test it on the real account first

The right shape is a sibling to the existing OTP-based `RecoverWhatsAppNumberDialog` (`D:/frontend-DA/components/recover-whatsapp-number-dialog.tsx`), which already proves the pattern of a focused dialog that walks a customer through one specific recovery action step-by-step rather than re-running onboarding wholesale. Concretely: a "Fix WhatsApp permissions" entry point (surfaced the same place the stuck-signup / number-recovery entry points already live, per commits `991d422` and the preceding "visible number recovery entry" work) that (1) tells the customer in plain language that Meta kept their WhatsApp number tied to a business they own but never finished setting up, and that a setting there is blocking template editing; (2) gives them our exact Business Portfolio ID to paste, mirroring 360dialog's and Wati's pattern of pre-identifying the partner ID rather than making the customer hunt for it; (3) links directly to `business.facebook.com` Business Settings with explicit click-by-click instructions (Accounts → WhatsApp Accounts → the WABA → Partners → Assign Partner → paste our ID → Full control → Assign), since no parameterized deep link into that screen is documented anywhere; and (4) ends with our backend re-polling `GET /{WABA_ID}/assigned_users` to confirm the task list actually changed, so the customer gets a real "fixed" confirmation rather than a bare "done" button — this verification call is the one piece we *can* run with our existing System User token, since reading our own assigned tasks doesn't require owner-level access. A secondary, clearly optional step should point toward Business Verification (Security Centre → Start Verification), framed as "do this too, for higher limits," not as required for the fix to work.

None of this should be built before a live test against the actual "Digital help" account, because the research converges on the same handful of unknowns that only a real login can resolve: whether the customer is in fact listed as Admin there; whether the Partners tab, given our *existing* partial grant, even presents a visibly different "Full control" state to select or looks like it's already maximally assigned; whether re-assigning actually changes `GET /assigned_users`' returned task list afterward; and whether this WABA is a Coexistence number, which would invalidate the whole Partners-tab approach in favor of a full Embedded Signup redo. Each of these is a five-minute check with the customer's own login and our own API token, and each one individually could redirect the design — for instance, if Coexistence applies, the "flow" collapses into "click the existing re-run-signup button we may already have," making this a much smaller build than a new dialog.

## Conclusion

The core shift in understanding from the prior research round is this: the permission gap was never a Meta policy wall with no key — it was a wall our app's own credentials can't reach past, while the customer's ordinary Facebook login almost certainly can, for free, with no token sharing and no Meta support ticket. That reframes this from "escalate to Meta and wait" to "ask the one person who can click a button to click it," which is both a smaller engineering lift and a faster resolution than anything the prior report's escalation path offered. The remaining risk is entirely empirical rather than architectural: if the live test shows the customer isn't actually Admin of "Digital help," or that re-granting doesn't move the needle on this specific already-partial-shared state, the self-service flow collapses back to the original escalation path — so the right next action is the fifteen-minute live check, not committing engineering time to the dialog yet.
