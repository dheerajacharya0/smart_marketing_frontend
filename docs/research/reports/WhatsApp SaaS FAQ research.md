# Answer the cost question before anyone asks

Converszio's best FAQ questions are the ones Indian buyers type when they are choosing a provider and are uneasy about cost: **"How much does the WhatsApp Business API cost in India?", "Is it free?", "Are customer-service replies still free after 1 October 2026?", "What does the tool cost on top of Meta?", "Can I keep my existing number and app?" and "Will my number get banned?"** Each one appears across three or more independent sources (competitor pricing FAQs, Quora titles, ranking article headings), and Converszio's positioning answers each of them directly: pay Meta directly, 0% markup, flat subscription, unlimited seats, coexistence, official Cloud API. Two of the seven FAQs live on the homepage today are now wrong. **"Can I switch?" promises Meta number migration, which Meta no longer supports on its new account model**, and "What will I pay Meta?" leaves out the **1 October 2026 change: service replies are free only for the first 1,000 per number per month, and utility templates are now charged even inside the 24-hour window** ([Meta: Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). How the FAQ is built matters as much as which questions it asks. **Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on every site on 7 May 2026** ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage)), and the best B2B SaaS study found FAQ schema has no effect on AI Overview citation ([Tripledart](https://www.tripledart.com/research/how-ai-overview-chooses-what-to-cite)). What does get cited is a question-shaped heading followed by a short, definite, fact-dense answer near the top of a page. So the work is to fix the two stale answers now, add about four high-intent questions to the homepage, and move topic FAQs onto dedicated pricing, India-cost, setup and comparison pages as those pages ship. One gap undercuts the rest: Converszio's own plan prices are visible only inside the app, so no search engine or AI engine can quote them.

## Six cost and number questions dominate what buyers ask

Pricing comes up more than anything else in every source type examined. Go4whatsup's India FAQ (updated 12 Nov 2026) opens with "How much does a WhatsApp Business API message cost in India?" and "Are customer-service replies still free?" ([Go4whatsup](https://go4whatsup.com/india/whatsapp-business-api-pricing/)). AiSensy's pricing FAQ leads with "What is the WhatsApp Service message pricing update effective October 1, 2026?" and "Which messages are still free?" ([AiSensy](https://www.aisensy.com/pricing)). RichAutomate asks "Is WhatsApp Business API free in India in 2026?" and "Does a ₹0 platform fee mean WhatsApp messages are free?" ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/is-whatsapp-business-api-free-in-india-2026)). Quora carries the same questions in older form: "What is the WhatsApp API pricing in India?" and "Which is the best WhatsApp business API service provider in India?" ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-WhatsApp-API-pricing-in-India), [Quora](https://www.quora.com/Which-is-the-best-WhatsApp-business-API-service-provider-in-India)). **Three vendors added a 1-October-2026 service-pricing question within weeks of the change**, so this question is current and still under-answered. Converszio can answer it with a primary source rather than a vendor blog.

Competitor FAQs confirm the table-stakes set. Across ten Indian platforms, **nine explain Meta's message charges, seven cover upgrade or downgrade, six cover trials, six cover cancellation, six cover prepaid versus postpaid, and four or more cover "can I use my existing number?"** (WATI, AiSensy, Interakt, WANotifier, DoubleTick, Gallabox, Zoko, BotSpace, Gupshup and Periskope pricing pages; see [WATI](https://www.wati.io/pricing/), [Interakt](https://www.interakt.shop/faqs/), [Zoko](https://www.zoko.io/pricing)). The buyer fears behind these questions are hidden markup, number bans from bulk sending, losing the WhatsApp app or the number, template rejection, green-tick eligibility and agent limits. An entire genre of "WATI alternative with no markup" pages exists ([Seekadu](https://www.seekadu.com/alternatives/wati), [WANotifier](https://wanotifier.com/?p=7689)). The ban fear is reinforced by national news reporting millions of Indian accounts banned every month, a large share of them for bulk or automated messaging ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/whatsapp-banned-97-million-indian-accounts-in-february-heres-why-470175-2025-04-01), [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/whatsapp-blocked-47-lakh-indian-accounts-in-march-16-lakh-of-them-proactively/1550882/)).

Competitors leave real gaps. **Nobody's FAQ answers GST on Meta charges, gives a worked rupee cost example, says what happens to unused wallet balance beyond "no refund", or answers "can I take my number and contacts if I leave?"** Several published answers are stale: AiSensy's homepage still says service conversations are free while its own pricing page says they are not, Interakt's FAQ still lists conversation-based rates, and DoubleTick still says "per conversation" ([AiSensy home](https://aisensy.com/), [Interakt](https://www.interakt.shop/faqs/), [DoubleTick](https://doubletick.io/pricing)). The markup claims also conflict with the published numbers. AiSensy quotes ₹1.09 per marketing message as "charged by Meta", and Interakt says "without any surcharge" while its per-message rates vary by plan ([AiSensy](https://aisensy.com/), [Interakt pricing](https://www.interakt.shop/pricing/)). Both sit above the ₹0.8631 base rate reported by every secondary source ([Mindlytics](https://mindlytics.in/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)). A dated, primary-sourced answer is the easiest way for Converszio to stand out, as long as it states exactly who bills Meta's charge, that there is no wallet, and how GST applies.

The research has limits. **Reddit could not be reached at all, and no live People Also Ask or search-volume data was captured**, so the ranking below is based on how many independent sources raise each question, weighted by how close the question is to a purchase. It is not based on search volume.

## FAQ rich results are gone; plain, early, quotable text is what gets cited

On Google, FAQPage markup no longer produces any visible result. **The feature stopped appearing on 7 May 2026, the documentation was removed on 15 June 2026, and Search Console support ends in August 2026.** Google says leftover markup "won't cause problems" ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/)). For AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google states "there's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add", and it asks only that structured data match the visible text ([Google: AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Tripledart measured FAQ schema on 19.5% of cited pages and 19.3% of skipped ones, which is no effect ([Tripledart](https://www.tripledart.com/research/how-ai-overview-chooses-what-to-cite)). Bing's Fabrice Canel has said schema helps Copilot's LLMs understand content ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-copilot-use-schema-for-its-llms-453455)). The right call is to keep the existing JSON-LD because it costs nothing, keep generating it from the single `lib/landing-faq.ts` source so it can never drift from the visible text, and spend the effort on the words themselves.

The measured evidence on citations points to form and position. In ChatGPT citations, **44.2% come from the first 30% of a document, cited text contains a question mark twice as often as uncited text (78.4% of those in headings), and definitional phrasing appears in 36.2% of cited text versus 20.2% of uncited** ([SEJ / Kevin Indig](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-science-of-how-ai-pays-attention/561306/)). In AI Overviews, 64% of citations come from the first 40% of a page, recency roughly doubles the citation rate, and a comparison table was the only on-page feature that stayed positive after controlling for rank ([Tripledart](https://www.tripledart.com/research/how-ai-overview-chooses-what-to-cite)). That has three consequences for this site. First, a homepage FAQ sits in the least-cited zone, so the key facts (0% markup, Meta billed directly, flat plans, 30-day trial) also belong in the hero or above the fold. Second, each answer's first sentence should be a complete, quotable statement, because that is what gets lifted. Third, the visible "last updated" date needs to change every quarter, since Meta revises rates only on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October ([Meta: Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)) and AI citations have a half-life of only 3.4 to 5.8 weeks ([Scrunch](https://scrunch.com/labs/citation-dynamics/)).

Placement needs one fix in the repo. The chat-style FAQ in `components/landing/faq.tsx` puts answers in the DOM only after a tap. The server HTML carries them only inside a collapsed `<details>` list in `app/page.tsx`. Google has said content hidden for UX gets full weight ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-on-myth-of-hidden-tab-content/358724/)), but a 2022 test disputed this ([SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/seo-content-tabs-hidden-google-rankings-33045.html)), and nobody has verified how AI crawlers treat collapsed text. Render the first sentence of each answer visibly, or open the list by default. As pricing, India-cost, setup and comparison pages ship, move each topic's questions onto its own page rather than building a thin /faq hub. Google's AI features expand one query into related subtopic searches ("query fan-out"), and a focused page answers each of those better than one long list ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

Keep FAQs factual, not evaluative. AI engines use a brand's own pages for definitional and pricing facts but not for "best X" recommendations. In one study, brand-owned pages led every citation pack for definitional queries and were absent for evaluative ones ([Previsible](https://previsible.com/seo-ai-news/how-llms-choose-brand-sources/)). In another, **69% of self-promotional "best" listicles had their own brand left out of the AI Overview recommendation** ([Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could)). Converszio gets recommended through third-party listings and reviews (G2, Capterra, comparison sites), not through a "best" claim on its own homepage.

## Conclusion

The FAQ opportunity here comes from timing more than from wording. On 1 October 2026 Meta changed which messages are free. Competitors' FAQs are visibly stale or self-contradictory about it, and the questions buyers ask most are exactly the ones a no-markup, no-wallet, pay-Meta-directly model answers most cleanly. Converszio can become the cited source for "is it free" and "what does it cost" by being the page that is current, dated and specific in rupees. The one thing blocking that is self-inflicted: with plan prices visible only in-app, the strongest positioning claim (flat price, 0% markup) has no number for an engine to quote. Publishing /pricing is likely worth more than any FAQ wording change.

There is also a maintenance cost to plan for. Meta revises rates every quarter, and AI citations turn over within weeks, so these answers need a quarterly refresh tied to Meta's 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October rate dates. `lib/landing-faq.ts` already pulls rates from `META_RATES_INR`. The same treatment should extend to the free-service-tier figure (1,000) and the starting messaging limit (250), so a single edit keeps every page, and the JSON-LD, truthful.
