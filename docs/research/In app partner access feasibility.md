# Meta blocks the in-app permission shortcut

**Not buildable, and the reason is structural rather than a missing feature or an App Review gap we could clear with more effort.** The proposed flow — fresh Facebook OAuth login from the customer through Converszio's own app, our backend taking that resulting personal access token and calling Meta's Graph API directly to grant our Tech Provider System User owner-level WABA permissions — fails two independent Meta gates at once, and clearing one does nothing for the other. The first gate is Standard Access: Meta's own documentation ties data visibility to whether the *target business* has formally "claimed" our app as an asset, a distinct admin action from logging in, which a customer's OAuth consent never performs on their behalf (`[Features Reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/features-reference/)`). The second, independent gate is that the one Graph API call capable of actually granting the permission — `POST /{WABA_ID}/assigned_users` — is documented as requiring a System User access token with admin permissions, not a personal token at all, meaning even a customer with perfect standing on the first gate would still be holding the wrong *kind* of credential for the second (`[Manage System Users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/solution-providers/manage-system-users/)`). A deliberate search for precedent — across BSP technical docs, Meta's own sample code, and open-source Embedded Signup implementations — found zero examples of anyone routing this specific grant through a customer token and a backend API call; every documented recovery flow, from 360dialog to Wati to Gupshup, hands control back to a Meta-hosted UI surface at the exact point the grant happens. This round does not change the recommendation from the prior research: the UI click-path (Business Settings → Partners tab → Assign Partner → Full control) remains the only working lever, and the right next step is still getting the customer to click it, not writing integration code against an endpoint that doesn't exist.

## Two gates stack, and a fresh login satisfies neither

The intuitive appeal of "just have the customer log in fresh and use their token" rests on an assumption that turns out to be false: that OAuth consent through our app and the customer's real-world admin status on their own business are the same kind of permission, or at least that one implies progress toward the other. Meta's Access Levels architecture treats them as orthogonal. Standard Access — the tier our app operates at without App Review — is documented as active "only for app users who have a role on the app or a role in a business portfolio that has **claimed** the app" (`[Features Reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/features-reference/)`), and "claiming" is a separate, affirmative action a business portfolio takes on an app asset through its own App Settings, not a side effect of a user clicking "Continue with Facebook" and accepting scopes. The customer being Admin of their own business — call it "Digital help," per the prior research round's working identification of the likely owner — does nothing to make that business have claimed *our* app. Nothing in a standard OAuth re-login flow performs that claim on the business's behalf, and no official Meta worked example describing this exact scenario (customer logs into partner app, does partner app gain Standard Access to the customer's business data) was found anywhere, so this is read from first principles of the documented mechanism rather than a single page that spells it out end to end.

Even stipulating the generous, unconfirmed case — that the customer's fresh login somehow cleared the Standard Access hurdle — a second, fully independent gate sits directly behind it. The actual write action needed, granting or upgrading our System User's task list on the WABA, runs through `POST /{WABA_ID}/assigned_users`, and Meta's current guide is explicit that this call needs "the access token of a System User with admin permissions" (`[Manage System Users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/solution-providers/manage-system-users/)`). That is a token-type requirement baked into the endpoint's contract, not a permission-scope question that a sufficiently-privileged personal token could satisfy by having the right OAuth scopes attached. The adjacent `owned_whatsapp_business_accounts` read endpoint carries the identical requirement — "an admin system user access token" (`[owned_whatsapp_business_accounts](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/reference/business/owned_whatsapp_business_accounts)`) — reinforcing that this isn't a one-off quirk of a single endpoint but a pattern across the whole post-Embedded-Signup management surface. The two gates are different in *kind*: one is about whose data our app's Standard Access reaches at all, the other is about which credential shape a specific write call accepts once you're past that question. A flow that fixes one does not touch the other, which is why "get a fresh token" cannot be patched into a working design by any combination of scopes, consent screens, or re-authentication — the token the customer could ever hand us through an OAuth popup is structurally never the token that endpoint will accept.

## No BSP, no sample repo, and no adjacent Meta asset type has built this

If an in-app, API-driven version of this grant were possible, it would be a natural thing for at least one of the half-dozen major WhatsApp BSPs to have built, since every one of them hits this exact recovery scenario regularly and all of them are already asking the customer to do *something* in a dialog. None have. 360dialog, Wati, Gupshup, and SleekFlow's documented recovery flows for a broken or insufficient partner grant all converge on one of exactly two sub-patterns: literal click-by-click Business Settings instructions with the BSP's Business/Partner ID pre-filled for copy-paste, or a button that re-triggers Meta's own Embedded Signup popup — which is still a Meta-hosted UI flow, not a backend API call against a stored customer token (`[360dialog BSP Permission](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/waba-basics/bsp-permission)`; `[Wati](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/13106135-how-to-add-wati-back-under-partners-in-meta-business-manager)`; `[Gupshup](https://support.gupshup.io/hc/en-us/articles/36261129567513-Guide-to-Revive-a-Failed-Go-Live-Gupshup-App)`). The same holds for open-source and sample code: Meta's own Embedded Signup implementation guide describes embedding the JS SDK and launching the popup specifically because the sharing/permission-grant step happens *inside* that popup by design, with no alternative API-only path offered (`[Embed the Signup Flow](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/embedded-signup/embed-the-flow)`), and the one self-hosted dashboard found on GitHub (ZeroMsg) simply wraps the same standard popup flow rather than bypassing it. No GitHub repository calling `assigned_users` or any comparable write endpoint with a customer-obtained token in place of the Embedded Signup popup turned up in search, though a dedicated GitHub code search (as opposed to general web search) wasn't run this round, so that absence is better treated as strong-but-not-exhaustive.

The comparison that settles whether this is a documentation gap or a real platform boundary comes from looking at Meta's other shareable business assets. Ad account partner-sharing — the asset class structurally closest to WABA sharing, since both are a single owner handing service-delivery access to one agency at a time — has a Graph API reference page that states plainly that create and update are **not supported** on `/{ad-account-id}/agencies`; only GET (list) and DELETE (remove) exist, with the actual grant explicitly routed to "the client goes to the ad account in their Business Settings and clicks Assign Partner" (`[Ad Account Agencies](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/reference/ad-account/agencies)`). That is a direct, documented "not supported," not silence — and it rules out the theory that Meta simply never got around to building a write API for this class of action. The counter-case proves the point further rather than undermining it: Meta Pixel sharing *does* have a full bilateral write API (`POST <ads_pixel>/agencies`, with a pending-request/accept flow that doesn't require UI on either side) (`[Business Pixel Sharing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/business-management-apis/business-asset-management/guides/business-pixel-sharing)`), which confirms Meta is technically capable of building exactly this kind of API when it chooses to. So the absence here isn't "Meta hasn't built it yet" — it's "Meta has, asset-by-asset, decided which sharing actions get a write API and which stay UI-gated," and WABAs landed on the same side of that line as ad accounts: both are single-owner, infrequently-shared, sensitive assets, and no POST endpoint for the initial partner-assignment step exists for either, versus pixels, which are shared constantly and bilaterally at scale and did get one. No Meta source states this design rationale in so many words; it is inferred from the pattern, not quoted from a changelog, but the pattern itself — read API present, write API absent, UI-only grant stated outright for the closest analog — is directly sourced and consistent across every asset checked.

## This is very likely a deliberate security boundary, not an oversight

It's worth being explicit about why this matters for how the team should think about the gap going forward, because "undocumented" and "deliberately withheld" call for different responses — one invites searching harder for a workaround, the other doesn't. The ad-account precedent being an explicit "not supported" statement, combined with the fact that the one Meta asset type that *does* get a write API (pixels) is also the one shared most casually and at the highest frequency across the platform, points toward a plausible and consistent rationale: Meta keeps the *initial* grant of control over a revenue- or compliance-sensitive business asset gated behind a human clicking a button in its own first-party UI, specifically because that action is rare, high-stakes, and the kind of thing you don't want a third-party app able to trigger programmatically off the back of an OAuth consent screen a customer may not read carefully. A WABA sharing grant and an ad-account partner grant both hand a third party meaningful operational control over something with real financial and compliance exposure; a pixel share hands a third party read/measurement access that gets set up constantly between advertisers and ad-tech vendors as a matter of course. Building a slicker OAuth-based flow on our side would not route around this; it would just be asking Meta's existing architecture to do something it was built specifically not to allow, through credentials (a personal token) that the relevant write endpoint is documented not to accept regardless of how it was obtained.

## The prior recommendation stands unchanged

Nothing in this round of research moves the needle on the conclusion from the earlier two reports, and it's worth saying plainly why it doesn't: those reports already established that the gap lives entirely in *our app's* relationship with Meta (Standard Access, no Advanced Access grant for `whatsapp_business_management` on a business that hasn't claimed our app), and that the customer's own login against Meta's first-party surfaces sidesteps that gap for free because it's evaluated on the customer's real Business role, not our app's access tier. This round asked the harder follow-up question — could we make our *own app* the instrument, using the customer's token, instead of sending them to Meta's own UI — and the answer is a clean no, for the two stacked reasons above, independent of whether our Advanced Access / App Review submission ever clears. Even in a hypothetical future where our app has full Advanced Access approved, the `assigned_users` write call still requires a System User admin token, which a customer's personal OAuth token will never be. The actionable next step is exactly what the prior report already specified: get the customer to open Business Settings → Accounts → WhatsApp Accounts → the WABA → Partners tab → Assign Partner, paste Converszio's Business Portfolio ID, check Full control, and click Assign — and build the recovery dialog (sibling to the existing OTP-based `RecoverWhatsAppNumberDialog`) around guiding them through that click-path, with our backend re-polling `GET /{WABA_ID}/assigned_users` afterward to confirm the grant actually landed, since that read call is one our existing System User token can already make. No engineering time should go toward an in-app API-driven grant path; the live checks still worth doing before building the dialog — whether the customer really is Admin of "Digital help," and whether this WABA is a Coexistence number that invalidates the Partners-tab approach entirely — are unchanged from before.

## Conclusion

The real finding here isn't that this particular flow is blocked — it's that it's blocked by two gates of entirely different character (a data-visibility rule about app-claiming, and a token-type contract on a specific write endpoint), which means there is no clever sequencing of OAuth scopes, re-logins, or App Review approvals that closes both at once; any fix for one leaves the other standing. Combined with the cross-asset comparison — ad accounts explicitly UI-only by Meta's own reference docs, pixels the lone counterexample with a real write API, and WABAs showing the identical read-API/no-write-API shape as ad accounts — the honest read is that Meta built this as a security boundary on purpose for exactly this class of asset, not as a gap it simply hasn't filled in yet. That reframes the decision in front of the team from "keep researching for an API path" to "treat the Partners-tab click as permanent product surface area": the recovery dialog should be designed assuming this human-in-the-loop step is never going away, rather than as a stopgap pending some future Graph API version.
